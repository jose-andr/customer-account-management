# Outcomes and value

Status: Draft
Owner: José Andrade
Last updated: 13 August 2026

## Purpose

Define the outcomes and value that Customer Account Management improvements should create, so prioritisation and future investment decisions can be based on evidence rather than activity or technology alone.

This page does not constitute a business case.

It defines the value hypotheses that should be tested through discovery, desktop research, analytics, design and controlled delivery.

## North star

> Assess the viability of improvements to Customer Account Management and potential future investment at City of Melbourne, using evidence to support prioritisation and business-case decisions.

## Value question

> If City of Melbourne improves Customer Account Management, what should become measurably better for customers, staff, the organisation and future service delivery?

## Outcome model

Customer Account Management value should be considered across four dimensions:

1. customer value;
2. operational value;
3. risk and governance value; and
4. strategic capability value.

An improvement does not need to create equal value across every dimension.

It should, however, have a clear and evidence-supported reason for progressing.

## Customer outcomes

### Desired outcome

Customers can interact with City of Melbourne with greater confidence that their identity, account relationships, information and preferences are handled consistently and appropriately.

### Potential value

Improvements may contribute to:

* fewer incorrect account relationships;
* less repetition when interacting across services;
* more consistent customer information;
* fewer inappropriate communications;
* clearer handling of customer preferences and restrictions;
* safer changes to customer information;
* improved continuity across interactions; and
* greater confidence in how customer information is managed.

### Evidence needed

Potential evidence may include:

* reported customer-impact examples;
* complaints or service-recovery evidence;
* incorrect account-linking incidents;
* repeated information requests;
* inappropriate communications;
* customer feedback;
* journey evidence; and
* validated operational examples.

Do not infer organisation-wide customer impact from isolated examples.

## Operational outcomes

### Desired outcome

Staff can identify, maintain and use customer-account information with less avoidable investigation, rework and uncertainty.

### Potential value

Improvements may contribute to:

* reduced duplicate-management effort;
* fewer manual corrections;
* less time spent identifying the correct customer record;
* clearer procedures;
* clearer ownership;
* reduced reliance on individual knowledge;
* fewer workarounds;
* improved handover context; and
* more reliable information for frontline and service teams.

### Evidence needed

Potential evidence may include:

* remediation volumes;
* duplicate-review effort;
* handling time;
* support requests;
* manual work queues;
* recurring escalation patterns;
* process observations;
* staff feedback; and
* repeat data-quality failures.

Operational efficiency claims should not be quantified without an agreed baseline.

## Risk and governance outcomes

### Desired outcome

Customer information is managed through clearer operational controls that reduce avoidable privacy, data-integrity, service and governance risk.

### Potential value

Improvements may contribute to:

* safer customer identity handling;
* reduced incorrect disclosure or account linking;
* clearer customer-data ownership;
* improved management of sensitive statuses;
* better handling of customer preferences;
* clearer correction and escalation pathways;
* stronger data-quality controls;
* better alignment between policy and operational practice; and
* more visible decision accountability.

### Evidence needed

Potential evidence may include:

* reported incidents;
* identified control gaps;
* audit findings;
* privacy or governance advice;
* unresolved ownership;
* exception volumes;
* data-quality failures; and
* control effectiveness evidence.

A reported risk is not the same as a confirmed breach or compliance failure.

## Strategic capability outcomes

### Desired outcome

City of Melbourne has reusable Customer Account Management capabilities that support current services and future connected customer interactions.

### Potential value

Improvements may contribute to:

* clearer customer-account standards;
* reusable identity and matching patterns;
* stronger customer-data stewardship;
* better quality monitoring;
* improved CRM confidence;
* clearer service-account relationships;
* more reliable customer context across channels;
* stronger foundations for digital and CRM change;
* safer use of analytics and AI; and
* improved readiness for future customer-experience transformation.

### Evidence needed

Potential evidence may include:

* reuse across multiple services;
* reduced duplication of local solutions;
* improved governance clarity;
* adoption of common standards;
* improved data-quality trends;
* reduced dependency on manual reconciliation; and
* use of shared patterns in future initiatives.

## Value versus solution

Value should be defined independently of a specific technology.

For example:

| Value sought                        | Do not assume                                              |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| Fewer duplicates                    | New master customer platform                               |
| Safer duplicate resolution          | Automated merging                                          |
| Better customer identity confidence | A single technical identifier solves identity              |
| Better data quality                 | Databricks alone fixes source data                         |
| Consistent account handling         | Salesforce configuration alone is sufficient               |
| Better governance                   | Additional policy documentation alone will change practice |
| Better customer continuity          | One centralised record is always appropriate               |

The preferred response may involve combinations of:

* process;
* governance;
* operating model;
* training;
* service design;
* CRM configuration;
* integration;
* analytics;
* automation; and
* technology investment.

## Value evidence hierarchy

Where possible, use stronger evidence before making investment claims.

### Level 1 — Reported signal

Examples, stakeholder reports or observed issues indicate potential value.

### Level 2 — Validated problem

The issue has been confirmed through current-state evidence, system behaviour or repeatable analysis.

### Level 3 — Quantified baseline

The scale, frequency, effort, affected population or risk exposure can be measured safely.

### Level 4 — Tested improvement

A controlled change demonstrates a measurable improvement.

### Level 5 — Sustained outcome

The improvement remains effective over time and creates reusable organisational capability.

A future business case should distinguish clearly between these levels.

## Business-case evidence signals

Deeper investment analysis may be justified where multiple forms of value are supported.

Potential signals include:

* material customer impact;
* repeated or systemic incidents;
* significant operational effort;
* recurring manual remediation;
* cross-service impact;
* substantial privacy or governance exposure;
* inability to resolve the problem through minor process improvement;
* dependencies affecting other strategic initiatives;
* measurable improvement potential; and
* evidence that a capability can be reused across services.

No single signal automatically justifies investment.

For each prioritised opportunity, record:

| Opportunity            | What improvement is being considered                   |

## Desktop scan contribution

The external desktop scan should strengthen or challenge the value hypotheses.

For each relevant example, ask:

* What problem was being solved?
* What value was expected?
* What evidence demonstrated that value?
* Was value primarily customer, operational, risk or strategic?
* What capability created the benefit?
* Was material technology investment required?
* What organisational conditions enabled success?
* What evidence is transferable to City of Melbourne?
* What should not be inferred?

The objective is not to prove that City of Melbourne should copy another organisation.

The objective is to improve the evidence used for prioritisation.

## Relationship to success measures

This page defines **what should become better**.

`02-define/success-measures.md` should define **how we would know** that it became better.

A measure should not be created solely because data is available.

Each future measure should connect to an explicit outcome or decision.
